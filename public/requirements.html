What you need to ride

RideWorld runs in your web browser: nothing to install. What works depends on the browser and the device, mainly for connecting your trainer and heart-rate strap. In short: Chrome or Edge on a computer works best, Chrome on an Android phone or tablet works with Bluetooth, and iPhones and iPads can only ride in the simulator.

Why

Bluetooth and USB in the browser

Talking to a trainer from a web page needs Web Bluetooth (wireless) or WebUSB (the ANT+ stick). Chrome and Edge have both; Firefox and Safari have neither. Apple makes every iPhone and iPad browser use Safari's engine, even Chrome, so none of them can reach your sensors.

The 3D scenery

The world is Google's photorealistic 3D model, streamed as you ride. It needs a graphics chip with WebGL 2 (any computer or phone from the last several years) and a steady connection: roughly 10-20 Mbit/s is comfortable. RideWorld lowers the detail by itself on slower machines; phones and tablets get a smaller memory budget so they don't run out.

The ANT+ stick on Windows

Windows lets one program own a USB device. For Chrome to use the ANT+ stick it needs the general WinUSB driver instead of Garmin's (one-off, with the free Zadig tool; there's a video in setup). Afterwards Garmin Express and Zwift's own ANT+ use need the original driver back.

Phones and tablets

They work well for riding, with a layout made for small screens and big buttons. Keep the phone plugged in: 3D uses a lot of battery. RideWorld keeps the screen awake while you ride. Tip: "Add to Home screen" opens it full screen like an app.

Ride photos with the whole screen

Photos that include the dials and maps use the browser's "share this tab" feature, which only computer versions of Chrome and Edge have. Elsewhere photos show the 3D view only.

Music

The YouTube Music player needs a big enough screen to stay visible, so it's hidden on phones; play music from the phone's own app instead.
